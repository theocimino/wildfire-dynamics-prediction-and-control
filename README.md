# 🌲 Wildfire Modeling, Prediction, and Optimal Control
## Abstract
Wildfire propagation is a critical environmental challenge driven by complex population dynamics. This repository models the interplay between forest density growth and stochastic fire outbreaks using continuous mathematics and discrete simulations. By generating large-scale synthetic data, we train Machine Learning models to predict fire risks and validate them against rigorous analytical frameworks. Finally, we formulate the firefighting strategy of "controlled burns" as an optimal control problem, dynamically reducing fuel continuity to prevent catastrophic mega-fires.   

## 🎯 Project Goals & Architecture
This project explores the complex dynamics of forest growth and randomly generated wildfires through a hybrid approach combining applied mathematics and artificial intelligence. The workflow is built around three main pillars:

- Mathematical Modeling & Simulation (0D & 2D):
Formulating and solving ODE systems (population dynamics) and 2D Cellular Automata / Finite Difference methods to simulate vegetation growth and spatio-temporal fire spread.

- Machine Learning & Analytics:
Generating large datasets of fire propagation to train predictive ML models (e.g., CNNs) for spatial risk assessment, and comparing their performance against analytical stability results.

- Optimal Control Strategy:
Implementing preventative strategies inspired by real-world firefighting. We optimize the timing and location of controlled preventative burns to break fire propagation dynamics and protect the ecosystem.

## Theoretical Mathematical Formulation

We consider $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ a two-dimensional spatial domain defined by coordinates $x$ and $y$, and a one-dimensional time domain defined by $t$. 
Due to the constraints of our chosen numerical approach (the Finite Difference Method), we require a simple spatial geometry. Therefore, $x$ and $y$ are defined within a rectangular or square domain, $\Omega = [-l,l]\times [-L,L] \quad \text{with }l,L \in \mathbb{R}$   
Thus we also consider the timespace as ${t \in [0,T]}$

We model the dynamics of two main state variables:
- $U(x,y,t)$ : the local normalized temperature.
- $S(x,y,t)$ : the forest density (or solid fuel mass fraction) at a given point in space.

---

## Heat Equation (Fire Propagation)


$\frac{\partial U}{\partial t} = k \Delta U - \vec{v} \cdot \nabla U + A S e^{-\frac{E}{RU}} - h (U - U_{amb})$

**Term interpretation:**
- $k \Delta U$ : Diffusion (heat radiation and conduction)
- $- \vec{v} \cdot \nabla U$ : Advection (wind effect)
- $A S e^{-\frac{E}{RU}}$ : Reaction (heat generated by combustion, following Arrhenius law)
- $- h (U - U_{amb})$ : Cooling (heat loss to the ambient environment)

---

## Fuel Equation (Forest Dynamics)

$\frac{\partial S}{\partial t} = rS(1-S) - C S e^{-\frac{E}{RU}}$

**Term interpretation:**
- $rS(1-S)$ : Natural forest regrowth (logistic growth)
- $- C S e^{-\frac{E}{RU}}$ : Fuel consumption (destruction by fire)    

Finally we get the following system :    
Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ be the spatial domain (e.g., a rectangular forest) and $\partial \Omega$ its boundary. The dynamics are governed by the following system:

```math
\begin{cases}
\frac{\partial U}{\partial t} = k \Delta U - \vec{v} \cdot \nabla U + A S e^{-\frac{E}{RU}} - h (U - U_{amb}) & \text{in } \Omega \times (0, T] \\
\frac{\partial S}{\partial t} = rS(1-S) - C S e^{-\frac{E}{RU}} & \text{in } \Omega \times (0, T] \\
\nabla U \cdot \vec{n} = 0 & \text{on } \partial \Omega \times (0, T] \\
U(x,y,0) = U_0(x,y), \quad S(x,y,0) = S_0(x,y) & \text{in } \Omega
\end{cases}
